### The Gist

There is a profound irony in the modern conflict with the petroleum industry. We treat oil almost exclusively as a **fuel**problem—a debate about tailpipes, carbon footprints, and geopolitical power. Yet, the same people protesting a pipeline are likely wearing, touching, and eating the industry’s byproducts. We have spent a century baking hydrocarbons into the "infrastructure of being" so deeply that we no longer recognize them as petroleum.

### The Conflict: Fuel vs. Feedstock

The public "war on oil" focuses on **combustion** (burning oil for energy), which accounts for roughly **85–90%** of a barrel of crude. The remaining **10–15%** is the "invisible world"—the feedstock for the petrochemical industry.

While we can technically swap a gas engine for a battery, we currently have no scalable, high-performance replacement for the molecular building blocks petroleum provides for:

- **Medicine:** Aspirin (derived from benzene), heart valves, and sterile IV bags.
    
- **Food Systems:** Synthetic fertilizers (Ammonia/Urea) and the wax coatings on produce.
    
- **Infrastructure:** The bitumen in the asphalt we drive on and the PVC in our plumbing.
    

### The Awareness Gap (The "Scanner" Perspective)

Most consumers suffer from **Material Blindness**. We recognize "oil" when it’s a black liquid or a price at the pump. We do not recognize "oil" when it is:

- A polyester shirt (Polyethylene terephthalate).
    
- A "clean" moisturizing gel (PEGs).
    
- A pair of yoga pants (Lycra/Spandex).
    
- The "Aroma" in a candle.
    

### The Irony of "Green" Modernity

The ultimate irony is that the transition to "green" energy currently requires a massive _injection_ of petroleum:

1. **Wind Turbines:** The blades are made of carbon-fiber-reinforced plastics (petrochemicals).
    
2. **Solar Panels:** Require plastic films and resin for protection and insulation.
    
3. **Electric Vehicles:** Are significantly lighter than steel cars because they use petroleum-based plastics and synthetic rubber.

### The Relatable Intellectual’s Take

We are like fish trying to describe the concept of "water." We are so immersed in a hydrocarbon-based reality that we think we can "divest" from oil by changing our car. In reality, unless you are standing naked in a forest eating wild berries, you are currently in a "handshake" with the petroleum industry.

The question isn't whether we should stop using oil for fuel—physics and climate data suggest we must. The question is: **Do we realize that even if we stop burning it, we will still be living inside of it?**
